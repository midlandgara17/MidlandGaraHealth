Understanding Hypertension

High blood pressure is common, often silent, and important to take seriously. Understanding what it means can help people recognise risk, monitor their blood pressure and seek appropriate care.

🩺

What is hypertension?

Hypertension is a condition in which blood pressure remains higher than recommended over time. Blood pressure is the force of blood pushing against the walls of the arteries.

❤️

Why does it matter?

Persistently high blood pressure can place extra strain on the heart and blood vessels and increase the risk of serious cardiovascular and kidney complications.

⚠️

Why is it called a silent condition?

Many people with hypertension have no obvious symptoms. A person can feel well while their blood pressure is elevated, which makes regular measurement important.

THE BIG IDEA

You can feel fine and still have high blood pressure.

One of the biggest challenges with hypertension is that it may not announce itself clearly. Headache, dizziness or other symptoms can occur for many reasons and cannot by themselves confirm hypertension.

The reliable way to know your blood pressure is to measure it correctly and, when necessary, have it assessed repeatedly by a healthcare professional.

BASIC CONCEPT

What do the two blood pressure numbers mean?

A blood pressure reading usually contains two numbers.

1

Systolic pressure

This is the top number. It represents the pressure in the arteries when the heart contracts and pumps blood.

2

Diastolic pressure

This is the bottom number. It represents the pressure in the arteries when the heart relaxes between beats.

For example, in a reading of 120/80 mmHg, 120 is the systolic pressure and 80 is the diastolic pressure.

Blood pressure classification depends on the guideline being used and on repeated measurements rather than a single isolated reading.

PHYSIOLOGY

How does the body control blood pressure?

Blood pressure is influenced by several systems working together. The heart pumps blood, blood vessels adjust their diameter, and the kidneys help regulate fluid and salt balance.

Hormonal and nervous-system signals also influence blood vessel tone, heart activity and the amount of fluid circulating in the body.

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

What happens when pressure stays high?

When blood pressure remains elevated over time, the increased mechanical stress can contribute to changes in the arteries and damage to organs supplied by those vessels.

The heart may have to work harder, while prolonged vascular stress can contribute to damage involving the brain, kidneys, eyes and other organs.

RISK FACTORS

Who is more likely to develop hypertension?

Risk can increase with age and family history, but hypertension can affect people across different age groups.

Modifiable factors

High salt intake, unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, excess body weight, tobacco use and harmful alcohol use can contribute to cardiovascular risk.

Non-modifiable factors

Age, family history and some underlying medical conditions can increase the likelihood of developing hypertension.

Secondary causes

Some cases of hypertension are related to another condition or to certain medicines. These cases require appropriate clinical assessment.

CLINICAL FEATURES

What symptoms can hypertension cause?

Hypertension frequently causes no noticeable symptoms. That is why relying on how you feel is not enough to determine whether your blood pressure is normal.

Very high blood pressure can sometimes occur with symptoms such as severe headache, visual disturbance, chest discomfort, shortness of breath, confusion or neurological symptoms. These symptoms require urgent medical assessment.

DIAGNOSIS

How is hypertension diagnosed?

Diagnosis is generally based on properly measured blood pressure readings, often repeated on different occasions.

The healthcare professional may also review medical history, medicines, lifestyle factors and other cardiovascular risk factors.

ASSESSMENT

What tests may be considered?

Depending on the individual situation, a healthcare professional may request investigations to look for cardiovascular risk, organ effects or possible secondary causes.

Blood tests

Tests may be used to assess kidney function, blood glucose, lipids and other relevant health factors.

Urine tests

Urine assessment can provide useful information about kidney health and other conditions.

Heart assessment

Depending on the clinical picture, tests such as an ECG or other cardiovascular assessments may be considered.

MANAGEMENT

How is hypertension managed?

Management depends on the person's blood pressure, overall cardiovascular risk, other health conditions and clinical assessment.

Healthy lifestyle measures are an important part of managing blood pressure. These can include a balanced diet, appropriate physical activity, maintaining a healthy weight, limiting excess salt, avoiding tobacco and moderating alcohol intake.

Some people also need prescribed blood-pressure medicines. Medicines should be selected and adjusted by an appropriate healthcare professional rather than started, stopped or changed without clinical advice.

MONITORING

Know your numbers.

Regular blood pressure monitoring can help identify persistent elevation and can help healthcare professionals assess whether a management plan is working.

When measuring blood pressure at home, use a validated device where possible and follow the manufacturer's instructions. Rest quietly before measurement and keep a record of readings if your healthcare professional recommends home monitoring.

COMPLICATIONS

Why should hypertension be taken seriously?

Poorly controlled hypertension over time can increase the risk of serious health problems.

❤️

Heart disease

Long-term high blood pressure can contribute to heart disease, heart failure and other cardiovascular problems.

🧠

Stroke

Hypertension is an important risk factor for stroke.

🫘

Kidney disease

Persistent high blood pressure can damage the blood vessels supplying the kidneys and contribute to chronic kidney disease.

👁️

Eye problems

Long-term vascular damage can affect the blood vessels of the eyes.

WHEN TO SEEK URGENT CARE

Don't ignore serious symptoms.

Seek urgent medical attention for symptoms such as severe chest pain, severe difficulty breathing, sudden weakness or numbness, difficulty speaking, sudden severe headache, confusion, seizures or sudden major changes in vision.
