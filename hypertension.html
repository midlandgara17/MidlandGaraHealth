Understanding Hypertension

A simple introduction to high blood pressure and why regular monitoring and appropriate medical care matter.

What is hypertension?

Hypertension, commonly called high blood pressure, means blood pressure is persistently higher than the level considered healthy. A blood-pressure reading has two numbers: systolic pressure and diastolic pressure.

Why does it matter?

High blood pressure can place extra strain on the heart and blood vessels. Over time, uncontrolled hypertension can increase the risk of serious cardiovascular and other health problems.

What can increase the risk?

  • Age and family history
  • High-salt or otherwise unhealthy diets
  • Physical inactivity
  • Excess body weight
  • Smoking and excessive alcohol use
  • Some medical conditions and medicines

Monitoring your blood pressure

Use a suitable monitor when possible and follow the device instructions. Rest quietly before measuring, keep the arm supported and take readings consistently. A single reading does not always tell the whole story.

When should you seek urgent care?

Seek urgent medical attention for severe or sudden symptoms such as severe chest pain, severe difficulty breathing, fainting, new weakness or numbness, confusion, seizures or other potentially life-threatening symptoms.

Important: This article is educational and does not diagnose or treat an individual condition. If you have repeated high readings or concerns about your health, speak with a qualified healthcare professional.
